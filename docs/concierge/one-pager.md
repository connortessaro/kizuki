# Kizuki: founding cohort

Kizuki is an intelligence layer over your work. It reads your Outlook mail and
calendar, GitHub, Jira/Confluence, and meeting transcripts, and tells you what
needs your attention before it bites.

## The problem it solves

You are the bottleneck. Most threads and meetings route through you, and the
thing that actually matters is always buried under the merely recent. You find
out a client went quiet three weeks too late. You walk into a call having
forgotten the one commitment that mattered. Search finds documents; it does not
tell you what changed or what to do about it.

## What it answers

Kizuki keeps a living understanding of your work and answers the questions
search and recall tools cannot:

- What changed?
- What matters now?
- What conflicts with what you understood before?
- What information is missing?
- Who is affected?
- What needs a decision?
- What should your AI agent know before it helps you?

Every answer carries a receipt back to the source. You start the day knowing
where things stand instead of reconstructing it from your inbox.

## What you get in the founding cohort

- A dedicated instance, set up for you.
- Onboarding: we connect three to five of your sources: Outlook (mail +
  calendar), GitHub, Jira/Confluence, meeting transcripts, plus Slack where your
  workspace allows it. We configure a Founder or Consultant Pack for how you
  actually work. Google mail/calendar support is on the roadmap; founding
  members influence the order.
- A weekly review call where we look at what it caught and tune it.
- Direct support from me, the person who builds it.

## What we never do

- Kizuki observes and advises. It never sends a message, changes a source
  system, or takes an action for you. You decide; it makes sure you decide well.
- It runs on your own dedicated instance. We don't want your data, and the
  design reflects that: your work stays yours.
- On exit, you can delete everything. No lock-in, no residue.
