# Kizuki Org tier — real-interest signal rubric

Status: draft, unvalidated — scoring reference for Task 4's VP/exec
conversations and Task 5's go/no-go decision. Independent of the pitch
one-pager (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-16-kizuki-org-tier-onepager.md`).

## Purpose

Score conversations after the fact, not during. Each criterion below is a
yes/no test with the polite-interest counter-example written directly next
to it, so scoring is a lookup against notes/quotes — not a judgment call
made in the moment or from memory of how the conversation "felt."

A criterion counts as **met** only if the contact raised it unprompted — if
you had to ask a leading question ("would this be useful for your team?",
"do you have budget for this?") to get the answer, it does not count, even
if the answer sounds positive.

## Criteria

### 1. Unprompted follow-up ask

**Test:** Did the contact ask some version of "what would it take to try
this" or "how would we get started" — without you asking whether they were
interested first?

- **Real:** "What would it take to get this running for my team?" / "How
  would a pilot with us actually work?" — asked on their own initiative.
- **Polite (not met):** They only respond when you ask directly — "would
  this be useful for you?" — with something like "yeah, could be." Or they
  say nothing that resembles a next-step question at all.

### 2. Concrete next step with a timeframe

**Test:** Did they commit to a specific action, with a specific timeframe,
without you asking them to commit to anything?

- **Real:** "Let me loop in my ops lead this month." / "Send me the
  technical one-pager for my security team by Friday and I'll get it in
  front of them next week."
- **Polite (not met):** "Sounds interesting, keep me posted." / "Send me
  more info and we'll see." / "Let's circle back sometime." — no named
  action, no date, or both left open.

### 3. Names a specific number, timeframe, or stakeholder unprompted

**Test:** Did they name a concrete number (budget range, headcount), a
concrete timeframe (a quarter, a date), or a specific internal stakeholder
(a named person or named team) when discussing next steps — without you
asking for specifics?

- **Real:** "We'd probably have budget for something like this in Q3." /
  "I'd want to run this by Jane, she owns our data governance review." /
  "Something in the $20-40k range wouldn't need extra sign-off."
- **Polite (not met):** "We might have some budget at some point." / "There
  could be people internally who'd care about this." / "Timing's always
  tricky, hard to say" — vague on number, timeframe, and stakeholder alike.

## General illustration

Real interest and polite interest can sound similarly enthusiastic in tone —
the distinction is specificity and initiative, not warmth. Example: real =
"send me a technical one-pager for my security team"; polite = "yeah AI is
definitely the future, we should chat again sometime."

## Scoring rule for Task 5

Mark each criterion **met** or **not met** per contact, based on notes/quotes
only. No criterion met, across all contacts = no-go. Any criterion clearly
met = go.
